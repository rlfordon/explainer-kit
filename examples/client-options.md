## The clause that decides it {#clause nav="Section 11"}

```stepper {title="How section 11 works" unit=Step next="Next step"}
1. **Either side may end the agreement.** Section 11 lets either party terminate "for convenience" with 90 days' written notice.
2. **Notice must be in writing, to the named address.** We sent it by courier and email to the notices address in section 14 on January 15.
3. **Deliveries continue during the notice period.** We kept ordering and paying through April 15, as the clause requires.
4. **Their argument.** Harbor Freight says a side letter from 2025 removed the termination right. We have not seen a signed copy, and the agreement says changes need both signatures.
```

```deeper {title="Go deeper: why the side letter matters"}
If a signed side letter exists and it removed section 11, our notice would not have ended the agreement, and their lost-profits claim would have a footing. That is why the first thing we need from you is every amendment and letter about the agreement.
```

```glossary
Term | Meaning
Demand letter | A formal letter stating a claim and what the sender wants, usually sent before a lawsuit.
Mediation | A negotiation run by a neutral third party. Nothing binds either side unless both sign an agreement.
Termination for convenience | A right to end a contract without having to show the other side did anything wrong.
Discovery | The stage of a lawsuit where each side must hand over relevant documents and answer questions.
```
