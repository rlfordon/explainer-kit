What Jev could do for legal research and citation analysis

Jev is a model from TypeSafe AI that writes nothing. It reads text, answers closed questions, and attaches a probability to each answer. Citator labels, cite-checks, and docket coding are closed questions. Here are eleven things worth trying, what supports each, and where the approach has already failed.

§01  How it works

Text in, typed answers out

A request has two parts. The state is whatever text you want judged: a passage, a docket entry, a JSON record. The questions are closed, and each declares its type. Every question in one request is answered in a single pass over the state, so ten questions take about as long as one.414

Think of Jev as a research assistant who fills in a coding sheet and never writes the memo. It cannot quote, explain, or search. Each answer comes back as a number your own code compares against a threshold, and the threshold decides whether a person looks.

choice
Picks one option from a list you define, and returns the probability of every option plus a confidence. Which treatment label fits this citing passage?
score
Places the text on ordered levels you define, as a probability-weighted position. Bare citation, a sentence or two, or extended discussion?
noul
Returns the probability that a yes/no statement is true. There is no separate confidence field, and you turn it into a boolean yourself.4 Does this passage support the proposition it is cited for?

Request

POST /v1/systemone
{ "model": "jev-1.13.0",
  "state": {
    "cited_case": "Doe v. Roe (hypothetical)",
    "citing_passage": "We decline to extend Doe beyond
      its facts. Unlike the plaintiff there, ..." },
  "questions": {
    "treatment": { "type": "choice",
      "instructions": "How does the passage treat the cited case?",
      "criteria": {
        "follows": "Applies its rule",
        "distinguishes": "Finds it inapplicable on facts or issue",
        "criticizes": "Disagrees but does not overrule",
        "overrules": "Declares it no longer good law",
        "unclear": "The passage does not show the treatment" } },
    "negative": { "type": "noul",
      "instructions": "Does the passage limit the
        cited case's authority?" } } }

Response

{ "answers": {

      "confidence": 0.78 },

§02  Why law fits

Legal research already runs on closed label sets

A citator signal, a Bluebook signal, and a docket event code are each a choice from a fixed list. The published benchmarks for legal AI are mostly the same shape, and the commercial products that assign these labels by hand disagree with each other more than most users assume.

Sources: LegalBench24, CaseHOLD25.

Source: Hellyer19.

Citator treatment phrases
followed by · distinguished by · criticized by · questioned by · limited · overruled by · superseded by statute
Bluebook Rule 1.2 signals27
[no signal] · e.g. · accord · see · see also · cf. · compare … with · contra · but see · but cf. · see generally
SCALES docket events26
complaint · removal · motion · order · judgment · verdict · sentence … about 27 events, 70+ labels in all

The Stanford study of commercial legal AI tools gave one failure a name. A misgrounded answer cites a real source that does not support the claim, and the authors call it harder to spot than an invented case. Lexis+ AI answered 65% of their 202 queries accurately and Westlaw AI-Assisted Research 42%.22 Whether a cited passage supports a proposition is a yes/no question about two pieces of text.

§03  Ideas

Eleven things to try

Only one of these has been built with Jev for law. Five borrow a pattern someone built with Jev in another field, and the rest are untested. Pick one to see the question you would ask, what supports the idea, and the main way it could go wrong.

Answer type
Evidence

Classify how a citing opinion treats a case

ChoiceNoulNot built

For every passage that cites a case, label the treatment. This is the core judgment inside a citator.

The question
choice: "How does the passage treat the cited case?"
  follows | distinguishes | criticizes | overrules | unclear
noul:   "Does the passage limit the cited case's authority?"
What supports it

The commercial citators missed or mislabeled 33% to 72% of clearly negative treatments in Hellyer's sample and agreed on the type 11% of the time19. Free Law Project is building an open citator for CourtListener, starting with Supreme Court overrulings20. General language models reached 79.1% on coarse labels and 67.7% on fine-grained ones across 239 expert-annotated citations21. Chandler names this as a use18.

Main risk

Keep the option list short and include an escape option. A 12-way Choice collapsed in testing8. Free Law Project's own scheme has "ambiguous" and "unverifiable" categories, so some share of passages has no right answer.

Check that a source supports the proposition

ChoiceBuilt elsewhere

For each sentence and the passage it cites, decide whether the passage supports it. This targets the misgrounded citation.

The question
choice: "A brief cites a case for `sentence`. `passage` is text from that case.
  How does `passage` relate to the claim made in `sentence`?"
  supports | contradicts | says_nothing
What supports it

citation-verifier does this for academic papers with nearly this wording. Claude finds the quote, code confirms the quote is verbatim in the source, Jev scores the relation, and rows under 0.8 go to a person16. The Stanford study defines misgrounded answers and calls them harder to spot than fabricated cases22.

Main risk

The citation-verifier's author states that its thresholds have not been validated on labeled data16. Legal support often turns on an inferential step, which is the difference between no signal and "see."

Audit Bluebook signals

ChoiceNot built

Given a proposition and the cited passage, pick the signal that fits, then compare it with the signal the author used.

The question
choice 1: "What is the relation between the text and the source?"
  support | comparison | contradiction | background
choice 2 (if support): states_it_directly | supports_with_inferential_step | supports_by_analogy
What supports it

Rule 1.2 defines each signal by the relation it asserts: no signal when the source directly states the proposition, "see" when there is an inferential step, "cf." for support by analogy, "contra" for direct contradiction27. That is a typed relation with an ordering by strength.

Main risk

Eleven signals exceed the reported 10-option cap and the tested limit for long lists, so the question has to be split in two829. Untested.

Sweep a corpus for overruling language

NoulNot built

Ask one yes/no question of every sentence that mentions another case: does this sentence say a case is overruled?

The question
noul: "Does this sentence state that an earlier decision is overruled,
  abrogated, or no longer good law?"
What supports it

The Overruling dataset is 2,400 sentences with attorney-annotated positives, and the paper reports F1 of 0.958 to 0.974 on it25. Free Law Project's first citator phase targets exactly this label20. A ready benchmark exists, so this is the cheapest idea to evaluate honestly.

Main risk

In Hellyer's sample the citators mislabeled one to three of four Supreme Court overrulings19. The hard cases are implicit: "overruling recognized by" and statutes that supersede.

Rerank search results for a research question

NoulScoreBuilt elsewhere

After a keyword or vector search returns candidates, score each one against the research question and reorder.

The question
noul: "Would this opinion excerpt be useful evidence for answering `question`?"
What supports it

A LlamaIndex adapter reports nDCG@5 rising from 0.340 to 0.396 on nfcorpus and 0.629 to 0.715 on SciFact at about $0.0003 per query, and it keeps the original order if Jev fails13. jev-reranker adds a cutoff that drops weak candidates36.

Main risk

The largest independent study found Jev alone slightly worse than an embedding ranker under independent labels, and better only when fused with it. No reranker rescues a candidate list that missed the relevant cases9. Score one row per request when order matters10.

Query dockets and opinions in plain English

NoulChoiceBuilt elsewhere

Add a natural-language predicate to SQL over a table of docket entries or opinions.

The question
SELECT * FROM docket_entries
WHERE court = 'cand' AND date_filed > '2025-01-01'
  AND jev(docket_entries, 'the court granted a motion to dismiss in full');
What supports it

jevql runs ordinary SQL first, then asks Jev about each surviving row and caches the answers. Extensions exist for SQLite, DuckDB, and Postgres123511. One reports 2,000 rows in 3.5 seconds for about $0.01211.

Main risk

Every such query is a full scan of the rows that survive the SQL filter. Accuracy drops with more than 20 to 25 rows per request11. The SQLite author warns that text in a row can steer the model, which matters when the rows are adversarial filings35.

Label docket entries without a training set

NoulNot built

Tag each docket entry with events such as motion, order, or judgment, and attributes such as granted or denied.

The question
One noul per label, all in the same request:
  "Is this entry a motion?"   "Is this entry an order?"
  "Does it grant relief in full?"   "Does it set a deadline?"
What supports it

SCALES already does this with a fine-tuned model over 70+ labels, pretrained on 11 million docket entries26. TypeSafe's guidance is one Noul per label when several can apply, and questions in a single request add little latency414. The appeal is a new label defined in a sentence rather than by retraining.

Main risk

A trained classifier may simply be better. In one test a naive Bayes baseline beat Jev on a 12-class task8. Compare against SCALES before building on this.

Forecast rulings on motions to dismiss

NoulBuilt for law

For each claim against each defendant, estimate the probability that the first written disposition dismisses it in full.

The question
noul: "Will the frozen claim against the identified defendant or defendant
  group be fully dismissed in that disposition?"
What supports it

LegalForecast-MTD is the only legal entry in awesome-jev. It was built by a practicing attorney, covers 91 cases, screens outcome-leaking text, requires a model's training cutoff to predate the decision, and scores probabilities with a Brier metric17. Its code calls the Jev API directly.

Main risk

No validated results have been published; the README says preliminary rows are withheld17. Older work sets expectations: 70.2% on Supreme Court outcomes30, a statistical model beating experts 75% to 59.1%31, and a summary-judgment model that only tied a baseline using party identity32.

Match parentheticals to holdings

ChoiceNot built

Given a citing context and a handful of candidate holding statements, pick the one the case stands for, or test whether a draft parenthetical matches.

The question
choice: "Which statement is the holding of the cited case as used here?"
  up to five candidate holdings, plus none_of_these
What supports it

CaseHOLD is 53,137 questions in exactly this form, five candidates each, where a legal-domain BERT reached 0.695 F125. The documented Jev examples use three options; five is untested.

Main risk

Untested with Jev. The candidates must come from somewhere, so this needs a generator or a headnote source upstream, and holding against dicta is contested even among people.

Code opinions for empirical research

ChoiceScoreNoulBuilt elsewhere

Fill in a coding sheet for thousands of opinions: outcome, standard of review, whether a doctrine was applied, how much discussion it got.

The question
Ten questions in one request. Put the codebook in the criteria:
  "criteria": { "applied": "The court used the test to decide the issue",
                "mentioned": "The court named the test without using it" }
What supports it

Several questions ride on one pass over the state, and one measurement found ten no slower than one14. A triage dashboard keeps every human correction and shows it to the model on later runs34. Chandler lists tabular review and research grids among the uses18.

Main risk

Score was the weakest type out of distribution, at 44.7% accuracy when the label depended on a policy missing from the text6. Report agreement with human coders the way any content analysis would.

Triage reference and intake questions

ChoiceNoulBuilt elsewhere

Route an incoming question to the right desk, flag urgency, and spot the legal issue area before a person picks it up.

The question
choice: "Which area of law does this question concern?"
  housing | family | consumer | employment | other
noul:   "Does the message mention a deadline within 14 days?"
What supports it

Routing is the most common pattern in awesome-jev, with low-confidence answers sent to human review1. LegalBench includes 16 binary issue-spotting tasks24. Chandler lists legal-aid intake18.

Main risk

Intake messages are confidential by nature, and zero data retention is an enterprise term318. A forced choice can also drop a qualifier that mattered, a failure commenters raised at launch29.

§04  Thresholds

Use the probability to decide who reads what

The projects that use Jev carefully all do the same thing with its numbers. Answers above one threshold pass, answers below another get flagged, and the middle goes to a person. One prose linter reports nothing at all between 0.4 and 0.6.15 The citation-verifier sends every row under 0.8 to human review.16

Below are 240 simulated cite-checks. Move the thresholds and watch who ends up reading what. Then switch the calibration: an independent test found Jev's probabilities well calibrated on familiar benchmarks but overconfident for Choice and underconfident for Noul on material it had not seen.6

The overconfident and underconfident settings use the refit temperatures (3.29 and 0.66) from one independent out-of-distribution test.6 Probabilities are rounded to two decimals, as the API does.7 That test's author also advises against thresholding on the confidence field, which never beat the top probability.

The jevcal project turns this into a routine: label at least 100 examples per question, choose the threshold on half, verify it on the other half, and fail the build when a model update breaks it.7 A second library adds separate enter and exit thresholds so an alert does not flap when a score hovers near the line.33

§05  Scale and cost

What a corpus-wide pass would cost

Seth Chandler's observation about citators is a claim about price: classifying how each citing opinion treats an earlier case becomes cheap enough to run across the whole CourtListener corpus.18 The arithmetic is simple enough to check. §06  Limits

Where it has already broken

No one has published an independent measurement of Jev on U.S. court opinions. These results come from other domains, and each maps onto a legal use.

32k tokens state plus longest question
Long opinions will not fit. You split them, ask the same question of each chunk, and write the rule that combines the answers. A tester's 53,000-character document only just fit.35
0.400 vs. 0.911 accuracy, one 12-way Choice vs. 14 narrow questions
Long option lists fail. On a 12-class accounting task, a single Choice scored 0.400. Having Jev score 14 narrow dimensions and fitting weights to them locally scored 0.911. A naive Bayes baseline on word pairs scored 0.949. Choice is reportedly capped at 10 options. Citator phrases, the eleven Bluebook signals, and SCALES's 70+ docket labels all need decomposing.829
0.932 → 0.579 rank agreement with the baseline, 1 row vs. 40 rows per request
Batching rows corrupts rankings. Packing 40 rows into one request moved 51% of yes/no probabilities by more than 0.20, and Score rankings fell further, from 0.974 to 0.506. A Postgres extension reports accuracy falling above 20 to 25 rows. Graded relevance fared worse than topic labels.1011
−0.028 NDCG@10 Jev alone vs. an embedding ranker
Reranking alone did not beat vector search under independent labels, across 9,831 graded pairs. Fusing the two rankings gained 0.090. The same study found that grading Jev with Jev-written labels flipped the sign.9
0.040 → 0.116 calibration error after adding qualifiers
Lawyerly questions score worse. Adding careful multi-clause qualifiers to a question raised calibration error and lowered agreement. A linter for question sets warns on compound questions, conditionals, and Choice lists with no escape option.514
44.7% at 0.74 accuracy vs. average stated probability
It cannot apply a rule it was not given. When the right Score depended on a policy absent from the text, accuracy fell to 44.7% while the model stayed confident. A local rule or a codebook has to be in the criteria.6
No passage no quote, no reasoning, no retrieval
Chandler: "A probability with no passage attached is hard to check and hard to defend."18 The citation-verifier pairs Jev with a language model that finds the quote and with code that confirms the quote is verbatim in the source.16
Confidentiality zero retention is an enterprise term
TypeSafe says it does not train on requests, and offers zero data retention to enterprise customers under a data processing agreement.3 Chandler, after reading the privacy policy, has "serious reservations about using Jev in representing a client or processing confidential information of any sort."18 Public opinions and dockets avoid the problem.
Rule 1.1 ABA Formal Opinion 512
Relying on a generative AI tool's output "without an appropriate degree of independent verification or review" can breach the duty of competence.28 A high probability can set the reading order. The lawyer still reads the case.

§07  First project

A cite-check gate for drafts, on public case law

This combines the two strongest precedents: the citation-verifier pipeline and jevcal's threshold routine. It is an inference from those projects. Nobody has published it for case law, and the thresholds would need a labeled set of legal citations before anyone relied on them.

StepWhat happensWho does it
1 · Extract and resolvePull every citation from the draft and confirm each case exists in CourtListener. A failed lookup is a possible fabrication.code
2 · Locate the passageFind the pin-cited page or the best candidate passage. Confirm any quoted language appears verbatim.search or LLM, then code
3 · Ask one ChoiceDoes the passage support, contradict, or say nothing about the sentence citing it?Jev
4 · Apply fitted bandsThresholds chosen on 100+ labeled pairs and verified on held-out pairs.code
5 · ReadA person reads everything outside the accept band, and samples inside it.lawyer or librarian

Damien Charlotin's database listed 2,045 court decisions involving hallucinated material on September 19, 2026, 1,397 of them in the United States.23 Step one addresses the invented case. Step three addresses the misgrounded one.
