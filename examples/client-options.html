Which route fits you?

Answer two questions
  • Do you need this resolved before the Q1 financing closes?
    • Yes
      Settle or mediate. A defended case will not finish in time, and lenders ask about open litigation.
    • No
      • Is keeping Harbor Freight as a supplier worth more than $50,000 to you?
        • Yes
          Mediate. It costs less than defending and keeps the relationship open.
        • No
          Defend. Our reading of section 11 is strong, and we can revisit settlement after discovery.

Our recommendation

Offer mediation and hold the settlement figure in reserve. Section 11 gives us a strong defence, and mediation tests their case for a fraction of the cost of litigation.

§03  Section 11

The clause that decides it

How section 11 works

Step 1 of 4

Either side may end the agreement.

Section 11 lets either party terminate "for convenience" with 90 days' written notice.

Step 2 of 4

Notice must be in writing, to the named address.

We sent it by courier and email to the notices address in section 14 on January 15.

Step 3 of 4

Deliveries continue during the notice period.

We kept ordering and paying through April 15, as the clause requires.

Step 4 of 4

Their argument.

Harbor Freight says a side letter from 2025 removed the termination right. We have not seen a signed copy, and the agreement says changes need both signatures.

Go deeper: why the side letter matters

If a signed side letter exists and it removed section 11, our notice would not have ended the agreement, and their lost-profits claim would have a footing. That is why the first thing we need from you is every amendment and letter about the agreement.

§04  Next steps

What we need from you

Next steps

Demand letter
A formal letter stating a claim and what the sender wants, usually sent before a lawsuit.
Mediation
A negotiation run by a neutral third party. Nothing binds either side unless both sign an agreement.
Termination for convenience
A right to end a contract without having to show the other side did anything wrong.
Discovery
The stage of a lawsuit where each side must hand over relevant documents and answer questions.
