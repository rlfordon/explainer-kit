FBI, CIA, NSA: who does what, and under what law

Three agencies work the same subjects — terrorism, foreign spies, cyber intrusions — under three different legal regimes. This page maps the boundaries: who each one may target, where, on whose approval, and who is watching.

FBI
Federal law enforcement and the country’s domestic intelligence service, inside the Justice Department.
CIA
Foreign human intelligence, all-source analysis, and covert action. No arrest powers.
NSA
Signals intelligence and cybersecurity, run as a Defense Department combat support agency.

§02  The model

Two questions divide the work

All three agencies work terrorism. All three work counterintelligence. All three work cyber. Subject matter does not divide them, which is why the popular shorthand of “FBI handles crime, CIA handles spies, NSA handles hacking” falls apart on contact with any real case.

Two questions do the dividing. The first is purpose: is this activity building evidence or producing intelligence? Evidence has to survive an American courtroom, so it is gathered under rules a judge will later test, and it must eventually be shown to the defense. Intelligence informs the President and policymakers. It never faces cross-examination, and it is often collected under authorities no court reviews.

The second is target: is the subject a US person? The term is statutory and covers citizens, lawful permanent residents, and US-incorporated entities, wherever in the world they happen to be. Nearly every restriction in American intelligence law attaches to that status rather than to geography.

Plot the two questions against each other and the division of labor becomes visible. The FBI occupies most of the space because it is the only one of the three that does both jobs. The CIA and NSA hold a single quadrant, and hold it with very few constraints.

§03  What people get wrong

Three claims worth correcting

Commonly saidThe CIA is banned from operating inside the United States.
Actually

The National Security Act denies the CIA police powers, subpoena power, law-enforcement powers, and internal security functions. It does not confine the agency to foreign soil. The CIA maintains domestic facilities, debriefs willing Americans, recruits foreign nationals who happen to be in the country, and works counterintelligence cases jointly with the FBI. The prohibition is on function, not on location.

Commonly saidThe NSA is forbidden from collecting Americans’ communications.
Actually

The NSA may not target a US person without an individual order from the FISA Court. It acquires Americans’ communications constantly by another route: when an American corresponds with a targeted foreigner, that message is collected too. This is called incidental collection, it is lawful, and the resulting communications are retained under minimization procedures.

Who may later search that retained pool, and on what showing, is the most contested question in US surveillance law.

Commonly saidThe FBI is a police force.
Actually

A large share of the FBI’s work has no prosecution in view. Its National Security Branch collects foreign intelligence and runs counterintelligence under FISA and Executive Order 12333, and those cases can run for years without a charge because a charge was never the point.

The United States has no separate domestic intelligence service in the mold of Britain’s MI5. The FBI does that job as well, which is why its authorities and its oversight are more tangled than either of the other two.

§04  The law

Five authorities do most of the work

Which one applies decides whether a judge is involved, what standard the government must meet, and whether anything collected can ever be used in court.

Title 18
The federal criminal code. Grand jury subpoenas, Rule 41 search warrants, Title III wiretaps. The government must show probable cause of a crime to an ordinary federal judge, and what it collects is eventually disclosed to the defense.
Title 50
War and national defense. The National Security Act of 1947 and FISA both live here. Authorizes collection for intelligence purposes with no prosecution required and, in places, no court involved.
Title 10
The armed forces. The NSA is a Defense Department combat support agency, so part of its work runs on military authority alongside US Cyber Command, whose commander has historically also directed the NSA.
Executive Order 12333 (1981)
The charter for intelligence collected outside the FISA framework, which in practice means most collection that happens overseas. It is a presidential order, so the President can amend it. No statute sets its limits and no court reviews collection under it.
FISA (1978)
Created the Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court. Title I orders require probable cause that the target is a foreign power or its agent. Section 702, added in 2008, permits warrantless targeting of non-US persons reasonably believed to be abroad using compelled help from US providers. Sections 704 and 705 cover US persons overseas.

§05  Oversight

Who watches, and what they cannot see

Judicial

The FISA Court

  • FISC — eleven sitting federal district judges designated by the Chief Justice, hearing applications in a closed courtroom with only the government present. FBI NSA
  • FISCR — the appellate court above it. It has issued a handful of public opinions in five decades.
  • Amici curiae — outside lawyers the court may appoint to argue against the government in novel cases, added by statute in 2015.

Legislative

Four committees

  • Senate and House Intelligence committees — receive covert action findings, hold classified briefings, and write the annual intelligence authorization. FBI CIA NSA
  • Judiciary committees — own the criminal-side statutes and FISA itself, which is why surveillance reauthorization fights run through them.
  • Appropriations — the budget is the leverage that actually bites.

Executive

Internal review

  • Inspectors General — each agency has one, plus a DoD IG over the NSA and an Intelligence Community IG across all of them. FBI CIA NSA
  • DOJ National Security Division — reviews and signs off on FISA applications before they reach the court.
  • PCLOB — an independent privacy board that has repeatedly lost its quorum, and cannot issue reports without one.

§06  Overlap

The seams where the three meet

A foreign plot aimed at a US city crosses all three jurisdictions in a single afternoon. Three institutions exist to manage that handoff.

Since 1980
Joint Terrorism Task Forces
FBI-led teams operating in field offices across the country, staffed by state and local police alongside federal agents. They are the mechanism by which a local traffic stop can reach the national counterterrorism system within hours.
2004
National Counterterrorism Center
Created by the Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act and placed under the DNI. It holds the government’s consolidated terrorism identities data and staffs analysts detailed from every agency, which is what makes cross-agency matching possible at all.
Removed 2001–02
“The wall”
A set of Justice Department practices that kept intelligence investigators and criminal prosecutors apart, so that FISA collection could not be used to build criminal cases. Section 218 of the PATRIOT Act and the FISA Court of Review’s 2002 decision in In re Sealed Case dismantled it.
Go deeper: why the wall still matters

With the wall gone, information gathered under intelligence authorities can inform criminal investigations. That is the point of the change, and the 9/11 Commission cited the separation as a failure that cost lives. It also produced the query fight: if the government holds Americans’ communications acquired without a criminal warrant, on what showing may an FBI agent search that pool for evidence of a crime? Every Section 702 reauthorization since 2018 has turned on that question.

§07  History

A century of law, mostly written after 1975

Spacing is proportional to elapsed time. The CIA and NSA had existed for decades before any statute governed what they could collect. Select a marker for detail.

  1. 19081908The Bureau of Investigation is foundedAttorney General Charles Bonaparte creates a small force of investigators inside the Justice Department, without congressional authorization. It is renamed the Federal Bureau of Investigation in 1935.
  2. 19471947The National Security Act creates the CIAThe same statute establishes the National Security Council and the Air Force. It gives the CIA its analytic and clandestine mandate and, in the same breath, denies it police, subpoena, and law-enforcement powers and internal security functions.
  3. 19521952The NSA is created in secretPresident Truman signs a classified memorandum consolidating military cryptologic work into a single agency. Its existence is not officially acknowledged for years, and it operates for a quarter century with no statutory charter.
  4. 19751975The Church CommitteeSenate investigators document decades of domestic spying, mail opening, and assassination plotting across all three agencies. The hearings produce the political conditions for every restraint that follows.
  5. 19781978FISA and the FISA CourtCongress requires a court order for electronic surveillance conducted inside the United States for foreign intelligence purposes, and creates a special court to issue them. It is the first statute to govern what the NSA may collect.
  6. 19811981Executive Order 12333President Reagan issues the charter for intelligence activities outside the FISA framework. Because it is an executive order rather than a statute, its limits are set and can be revised by the President.
  7. 20012001The PATRIOT ActPassed six weeks after 11 September. Section 218 lets the government use FISA when foreign intelligence is a significant purpose rather than the primary purpose, which is the change that brings down the wall between intelligence and criminal work.
  8. 20042004Intelligence reform creates the DNIThe Intelligence Reform and Terrorism Prevention Act, following the 9/11 Commission, establishes a Director of National Intelligence over the whole community and creates the National Counterterrorism Center.
  9. 20082008Section 702 is added to FISAThe FISA Amendments Act authorizes warrantless targeting of non-US persons abroad using compelled assistance from US providers, replacing the warrantless program run outside the statute after 2001.
  10. 20132013The Snowden disclosuresLeaked documents describe bulk telephone metadata collection and the scale of §702, triggering the first serious public debate about surveillance authorities since 1978.
  11. 20152015The USA FREEDOM ActEnds bulk collection of domestic telephone metadata under Section 215, requires the FISA Court to appoint outside amici in novel cases, and mandates declassification of significant opinions.
  12. 20242024RISAA reauthorizes §702The Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act extends Section 702 for two years, adds query training and accountability requirements for FBI personnel, and declines to impose a general warrant requirement.
  13. 20262026Section 702 lapsesAfter a series of short extensions, the statutory authority expires on 12 June 2026 with no reauthorization in place. Collection continues under FISA Court certifications approved in March 2026 and valid into March 2027.

§08  Section 702

Section 702 lapsed in June 2026, and collection continued

Fast-moving · verify before relying on this

Section 702 is the authority that lets the NSA compel American communications providers to hand over the messages of foreign targets abroad. It is also where Americans’ communications enter the system incidentally, which is why every reauthorization becomes a fight about the FBI’s ability to query what has already been collected.

Congress reauthorized it in April 2024 through the Reforming Intelligence and Securing America Act, with a two-year sunset. That sunset arrived in April 2026 and was pushed back by a series of short extensions while negotiations continued. The statutory authority expired at midnight on 12 June 2026 without a new reauthorization.

Collection did not stop. Section 702 runs on annual certifications approved by the FISA Court, and the certifications approved in March 2026 remain valid into March 2027. The lapse ended the government’s ability to issue new certifications; it did not switch off the programs operating under existing ones.

That gap between the statute and the certifications is what makes the current moment unusual. It also means the practical deadline for Congress is the certification expiry, not the sunset date that already passed.

§09  Quiz

Five questions on the distinctions that matter

5 questions
  1. Which of the three can arrest someone inside the United States?
    Why

    Arrest authority sits with the FBI alone. The National Security Act expressly denies the CIA police and law-enforcement powers, and the NSA has never had them. A CIA or NSA lead that needs an arrest has to hand the case to the FBI.

  2. The NSA targets a foreign official abroad and collects an email they sent to an American in Ohio. What is the status of that email?
    Why

    This is incidental collection, and it is lawful under Section 702 because the target was the foreigner, not the American. The email is retained rather than destroyed. The contested question is who may later search that repository for the American, and on what showing.

  3. What authority covers most CIA and NSA collection conducted overseas?
    Why

    Executive Order 12333 governs intelligence activity outside the FISA framework, which is most overseas collection. It is a presidential order, not a statute, so no court reviews collection under it and the President can amend its terms.

  4. Under Section 702, what does the FISA Court actually approve?
    Why

    The court reviews the program once a year and certifies the procedures. Analysts then select individual targets themselves by applying those procedures. That programmatic rather than individualized approval is the structural feature critics object to.

  5. The government wants to target an American living in Berlin for foreign intelligence. What does it need?
    Why

    US-person status travels with the person. Section 704 requires an individualized FISA Court order to target an American abroad, even though collection against the foreign nationals around them needs no court approval at all.
