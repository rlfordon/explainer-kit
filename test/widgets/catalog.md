## Eleven things to try {nav="Split"}

Only one of these has been built with Jev for law. Five borrow a pattern someone built with Jev in another field, and the rest are untested. Pick one to see the question you would ask, what supports the idea, and the main way it could go wrong.

```catalog {.split noun="ideas" empty="No idea matches both filters. Clear a filter to see one."}
Idea | What it is | Answer type:facet {order="Choice; Score; Noul"} | Evidence:facet {.dot order="Built for law; Built elsewhere; Not built"} | The question:code | What supports it:detail | Main risk:detail {.warn}
Classify how a citing opinion treats a case | For every passage that cites a case, label the treatment. This is the core judgment inside a citator. | Choice, Noul | [Not built]{.untried} | choice: "How does the passage treat the cited case?"\n  follows \| distinguishes \| criticizes \| overrules \| unclear\nnoul:   "Does the passage limit the cited case's authority?" | The commercial citators missed or mislabeled 33% to 72% of clearly negative treatments in Hellyer's sample and agreed on the type 11% of the time [19](#s19). Free Law Project is building an open citator for CourtListener, starting with Supreme Court overrulings [20](#s20). General language models reached 79.1% on coarse labels and 67.7% on fine-grained ones across 239 expert-annotated citations [21](#s21). Chandler names this as a use [18](#s18). | Keep the option list short and include an escape option. A 12-way Choice collapsed in testing [8](#s8). Free Law Project's own scheme has "ambiguous" and "unverifiable" categories, so some share of passages has no right answer.
Check that a source supports the proposition | For each sentence and the passage it cites, decide whether the passage supports it. This targets the misgrounded citation. | Choice | [Built elsewhere]{.elsewhere} | choice: "A brief cites a case for `sentence`. `passage` is text from that case.\n  How does `passage` relate to the claim made in `sentence`?"\n  supports \| contradicts \| says_nothing | citation-verifier does this for academic papers with nearly this wording. Claude finds the quote, code confirms the quote is verbatim in the source, Jev scores the relation, and rows under 0.8 go to a person [16](#s16). The Stanford study defines misgrounded answers and calls them harder to spot than fabricated cases [22](#s22). | The citation-verifier's author states that its thresholds have not been validated on labeled data [16](#s16). Legal support often turns on an inferential step, which is the difference between no signal and "see."
Audit Bluebook signals | Given a proposition and the cited passage, pick the signal that fits, then compare it with the signal the author used. | Choice | [Not built]{.untried} | choice 1: "What is the relation between the text and the source?"\n  support \| comparison \| contradiction \| background\nchoice 2 (if support): states_it_directly \| supports_with_inferential_step \| supports_by_analogy | Rule 1.2 defines each signal by the relation it asserts: no signal when the source directly states the proposition, "see" when there is an inferential step, "cf." for support by analogy, "contra" for direct contradiction [27](#s27). That is a typed relation with an ordering by strength. | Eleven signals exceed the reported 10-option cap and the tested limit for long lists, so the question has to be split in two [8](#s8) [29](#s29). Untested.
Sweep a corpus for overruling language | Ask one yes/no question of every sentence that mentions another case: does this sentence say a case is overruled? | Noul | [Not built]{.untried} | noul: "Does this sentence state that an earlier decision is overruled,\n  abrogated, or no longer good law?" | The Overruling dataset is 2,400 sentences with attorney-annotated positives, and the paper reports F1 of 0.958 to 0.974 on it [25](#s25). Free Law Project's first citator phase targets exactly this label [20](#s20). A ready benchmark exists, so this is the cheapest idea to evaluate honestly. | In Hellyer's sample the citators mislabeled one to three of four Supreme Court overrulings [19](#s19). The hard cases are implicit: "overruling recognized by" and statutes that supersede.
Rerank search results for a research question | After a keyword or vector search returns candidates, score each one against the research question and reorder. | Noul, Score | [Built elsewhere]{.elsewhere} | noul: "Would this opinion excerpt be useful evidence for answering `question`?" | A LlamaIndex adapter reports nDCG@5 rising from 0.340 to 0.396 on nfcorpus and 0.629 to 0.715 on SciFact at about $0.0003 per query, and it keeps the original order if Jev fails [13](#s13). jev-reranker adds a cutoff that drops weak candidates [36](#s36). | The largest independent study found Jev alone slightly worse than an embedding ranker under independent labels, and better only when fused with it. No reranker rescues a candidate list that missed the relevant cases [9](#s9). Score one row per request when order matters [10](#s10).
Query dockets and opinions in plain English | Add a natural-language predicate to SQL over a table of docket entries or opinions. | Noul, Choice | [Built elsewhere]{.elsewhere} | SELECT * FROM docket_entries\nWHERE court = 'cand' AND date_filed > '2025-01-01'\n  AND jev(docket_entries, 'the court granted a motion to dismiss in full'); | jevql runs ordinary SQL first, then asks Jev about each surviving row and caches the answers. Extensions exist for SQLite, DuckDB, and Postgres [12](#s12) [35](#s35) [11](#s11). One reports 2,000 rows in 3.5 seconds for about $0.012 [11](#s11). | Every such query is a full scan of the rows that survive the SQL filter. Accuracy drops with more than 20 to 25 rows per request [11](#s11). The SQLite author warns that text in a row can steer the model, which matters when the rows are adversarial filings [35](#s35).
Label docket entries without a training set | Tag each docket entry with events such as motion, order, or judgment, and attributes such as granted or denied. | Noul | [Not built]{.untried} | One noul per label, all in the same request:\n  "Is this entry a motion?"   "Is this entry an order?"\n  "Does it grant relief in full?"   "Does it set a deadline?" | SCALES already does this with a fine-tuned model over 70+ labels, pretrained on 11 million docket entries [26](#s26). TypeSafe's guidance is one Noul per label when several can apply, and questions in a single request add little latency [4](#s4) [14](#s14). The appeal is a new label defined in a sentence rather than by retraining. | A trained classifier may simply be better. In one test a naive Bayes baseline beat Jev on a 12-class task [8](#s8). Compare against SCALES before building on this.
Forecast rulings on motions to dismiss | For each claim against each defendant, estimate the probability that the first written disposition dismisses it in full. | Noul | [Built for law]{.law} | noul: "Will the frozen claim against the identified defendant or defendant\n  group be fully dismissed in that disposition?" | LegalForecast-MTD is the only legal entry in awesome-jev. It was built by a practicing attorney, covers 91 cases, screens outcome-leaking text, requires a model's training cutoff to predate the decision, and scores probabilities with a Brier metric [17](#s17). Its code calls the Jev API directly. | No validated results have been published; the README says preliminary rows are withheld [17](#s17). Older work sets expectations: 70.2% on Supreme Court outcomes [30](#s30), a statistical model beating experts 75% to 59.1% [31](#s31), and a summary-judgment model that only tied a baseline using party identity [32](#s32).
Match parentheticals to holdings | Given a citing context and a handful of candidate holding statements, pick the one the case stands for, or test whether a draft parenthetical matches. | Choice | [Not built]{.untried} | choice: "Which statement is the holding of the cited case as used here?"\n  up to five candidate holdings, plus none_of_these | CaseHOLD is 53,137 questions in exactly this form, five candidates each, where a legal-domain BERT reached 0.695 F1 [25](#s25). The documented Jev examples use three options; five is untested. | Untested with Jev. The candidates must come from somewhere, so this needs a generator or a headnote source upstream, and holding against dicta is contested even among people.
Code opinions for empirical research | Fill in a coding sheet for thousands of opinions: outcome, standard of review, whether a doctrine was applied, how much discussion it got. | Choice, Score, Noul | [Built elsewhere]{.elsewhere} | Ten questions in one request. Put the codebook in the criteria:\n  "criteria": { "applied": "The court used the test to decide the issue",\n                "mentioned": "The court named the test without using it" } | Several questions ride on one pass over the state, and one measurement found ten no slower than one [14](#s14). A triage dashboard keeps every human correction and shows it to the model on later runs [34](#s34). Chandler lists tabular review and research grids among the uses [18](#s18). | Score was the weakest type out of distribution, at 44.7% accuracy when the label depended on a policy missing from the text [6](#s6). Report agreement with human coders the way any content analysis would.
Triage reference and intake questions | Route an incoming question to the right desk, flag urgency, and spot the legal issue area before a person picks it up. | Choice, Noul | [Built elsewhere]{.elsewhere} | choice: "Which area of law does this question concern?"\n  housing \| family \| consumer \| employment \| other\nnoul:   "Does the message mention a deadline within 14 days?" | Routing is the most common pattern in awesome-jev, with low-confidence answers sent to human review [1](#s1). LegalBench includes 16 binary issue-spotting tasks [24](#s24). Chandler lists legal-aid intake [18](#s18). | Intake messages are confidential by nature, and zero data retention is an enterprise term [3](#s3) [18](#s18). A forced choice can also drop a qualifier that mattered, a failure commenters raised at launch [29](#s29).
```

## Patterns, filtered by lever and by evidence {nav="Rows" .band}

Each row opens to a sketch, the evidence behind it, and where it breaks. Lever filters keep only the patterns that use every lever you pick.

```catalog {search=yes noun="patterns" empty="No pattern uses every selected lever with that evidence. Clear a filter."}
Pattern | What it does | Family:group | Lever:facet {.all .pips order="Nearly free; Fast; Distributions; Many at once"} | Evidence:facet {.multi order="Author-tested; TypeSafe docs; Community; Untried"} | Sketch:detail | Evidence:detail | Breaks when:detail {.warn}
Quick profile | Ask 10–20 questions of one document in one request and get its profile: kind, posture, who wins, whether it reaches the issue, how deep. | Get a read | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Many at once]{.many} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | state = one document or window; questions = a battery of Nouls and Choices; code renders a profile card. | Podcast Signal's episode preview is in production and was right on everything checked. Four questions over 209 citing cases cost $0.0165. | Questions that need inference across a whole long document; distractor-heavy state.
Speculative fan-out | Ask every question you might need in one request; code reads only the branch it takes. | Get a read | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Many at once]{.many} | TypeSafe docs | One request, all branches of the decision tree as named questions. | TypeSafe's parallel-questions cookbook: 12.2× cheaper and 10× faster than one question per call. | Answers shift slightly with the other questions in the request, so version the question set.
Two-threshold funnel | Clear the confident yeses, flag the confident noes, send only the middle band to an LLM or a person. | Cut a big set down | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Distributions]{.dist} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | One precise Noul, plus code checks first; thresholds fitted on held-out labels. | Held out: 36 of 154 claims cleared with 0 bad; 19 flagged with 0 false accusations. 64% still went to the LLM. | A single 0.5 cutoff, or thresholds fitted in-sample. Flag recall was only 36–43%.
Census with an audit | When the universe can be listed, score all of it on one coarse question and audit a random sample for the miss rate. | Cut a big set down | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Fast]{.fast} | [Untried]{.muted} | Enumerate (e.g. every circuit opinion matching a broad query); one Noul each; random audit of the negatives. | Closest run: 832 opinions on five questions in 22.3 s for $0.074; the 23 flagged held every known candidate. | Only as complete as the query that listed the universe. The audit is the expensive part.
Citator-style treatment screen | Over each citing case's citation-context window: followed, distinguished, limited, overruled? | Cut a big set down | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Many at once]{.many} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | state = ±1,500 characters around the citation; a treatment Choice plus a "no longer good law" Noul. | 1,067 citers of three overruled Supreme Court cases in 26.7 s for $0.092. No-longer-good-law at ≥0.5: recall 0.965, precision 0.974. Haiku's recall was 0.83. | Overruling that must be inferred: it missed Juliana and the Ramos concurrence.
Two-stage narrowing | First request: a Choice over every option's short description. Second: the top three with full text, and an absolute "fits?" Noul for each. | Cut a big set down | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Many at once]{.many} | [Community]{.warn} | Request 2's state exists only after request 1 answers; that is the only reason for a second call. | TypeSafe's skill-suggestion cookbook over 182 skills; community skill routers for Claude Code. [Project](https://github.com/shimo4228/jev-skill-router) | One user called a personal prompt router "OK at best."
Question tree in one call | Encode a decision tree as parallel questions; code walks it. The tree doubles as the issue's fact-pattern schema. | Taxonomies and structure | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Many at once]{.many} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | Every node a named question; code derives composite fields from the walk. | Eleven questions over 72 opinions: 2.2 s, under a cent. Agreement with Sonnet: 74% on whether entry occurred, 70% on who initiated, 54% on entry mode. | Attributes that depend on what the excerpt window caught.
Beam search down a big taxonomy | Past 255 options, descend one Choice per level, keep the top K paths, score each path by the geometric mean of its probabilities. | Taxonomies and structure | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Distributions]{.dist}, [Many at once]{.many} | TypeSafe docs | Candidate uses: subject headings, Key Number outlines, research-guide tagging. | Hierarchical-classification cookbook: beam K=3 got 4 of 4, greedy 2 of 4. | Opaque option keys. Always send the child's label, not just its subtree.
Taxonomy debugger | Run a draft taxonomy over a few hundred items and read the distributions: categories that keep splitting overlap, a category that never wins is dead. | Taxonomies and structure | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Distributions]{.dist} | [Untried]{.muted} | One Choice per item; code tallies top-two splits and win counts. | Untried. The demo uses synthetic data. | Overlap can come from vague criteria rather than a bad category. Rewrite the definitions before merging.
Uncertainty spotlight | Spend human reading where Jev is least sure. The 0.3–0.7 band collects the contested, partial and badly worded items. | Probabilities as data | [Distributions]{.dist} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | Sort by distance from 0.5, or by Choice margin, instead of by score. | Every claim at 0.95 or above was supported and none under 0.50 was; "partial" claims sat in the middle. | Out-of-distribution overconfidence can push hard items to the extremes.
Disagreement mining | Ask a broad and a narrow version of a question, or compare Jev with an LLM; read where they diverge. | Probabilities as data | [Distributions]{.dist}, [Many at once]{.many} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | Two questions in the same request; code flags large gaps. | R5 follow-up: the broad-versus-narrow gap kept all 9 must-have documents. One "false alarm" (Gibbons) was Jev right and the Sonnet label too narrow. | The two questions have to measure different things.
Search over wordings | Asking is nearly free, so try many phrasings against a labeled set and keep the one that separates best. | Probabilities as data | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Distributions]{.dist} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | A bank of candidate wordings; score each on the same labeled claims; lockbox held out. | Phrasing loop: lockbox clears went from 22 to 30 with 0 bad. At 0.50, the old wording cleared 77 with 13 bad; the new one 38 with 0 bad. | Overfitting: the nearest bad claim sat at 0.250 against a 0.255 threshold.
Locator | One Choice over numbered passages: which one decides X? Jev cannot quote, but it can pick. | Making it point | [Many at once]{.many} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | Split the opinion into ≤250 passages; keys are passage IDs; add none. | Right passage first 77%, top three 92%, top five 96%. Top five plus neighbours is 27% of the opinion. | 255-option cap; long opinions need splitting.
Remove-a-sentence attribution | Delete one sentence at a time and re-ask. The sentences whose removal moves the answer most are what drove it. | Making it point | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Fast]{.fast} | [Untried]{.muted} | N+1 requests per passage; rank sentences by how far the answer moves. | Untried. | Redundant evidence hides: removing one of two supporting sentences moves nothing.
Pairwise judgment | Put both sides in one state: claim and passage, query and candidate, version A and version B. | Two texts at once | [Many at once]{.many} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | state = {a, b}; several questions per pair. | The citation gate is pairwise (proposition + passage). Rerank cookbook on legal queries: top-10 hit rate from 38% to 62%, 1,200 calls for $0.0645. | Generic record dedupe was "underwhelming" in one field report.
Check, then escalate | A Noul battery checks each claim or field of an LLM's output; any flag over a bar escalates to a stronger model. | Watching processes | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Distributions]{.dist} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | Per-field questions such as field::hallucinated; escalate if any P(wrong) > 0.7. | A holistic "is this good?" judge gave 0.56 where per-field checks gave 0.95 and 0.85. The citation checker caught a real wrong cite: 133 N.J. 347, not 247. | Vague "anything left out?" questions scored 0.78–0.90 on everything.
Agent tripwires | Inside a long agent run, ask at each step: still on task? does this draft answer the question? is the work actually done? | Watching processes | [Nearly free]{.cheap}, [Fast]{.fast} | [Community]{.warn} | Hook fires per turn; state = task plus latest output; Noul gate. | JevFlow asks Jev whether a phase is really done before Claude may stop; agent-chaperone screens tool calls and results. [Project](https://github.com/Parth1811/JevFlow) | Not a security boundary: injected text in the state is not treated as hostile.
Control loop | State in, Choice over the legal actions out, code executes, repeat every tick. | Live and in the loop | [Fast]{.fast}, [Many at once]{.many} | [Community]{.warn} | Keys = the actions allowed right now; code enforces rules. | Community projects drive a browser and play Pokémon Red this way. [Project](https://github.com/milanboers/jev-plays-pokemon) | Search-like tasks fail: it blundered on every chess move.
Feed triage | New reports, opinions or episodes: is this in my interests, and which project does it belong to? | Live and in the loop | [Nearly free]{.cheap} | [Author-tested]{.ok} | Absolute interest Noul plus a project Choice with none. | Podcast Signal preview in production. Untried for CRS reports and opinion alerts. | A title alone carries little evidence.
```
